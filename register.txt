1. KERNEL

OBJECTIVE: Establish model and its principal results in canonical, compressed LLM-directed form.
INPUT: Model specifications, mathematical notes, derivations, and research findings.
OUTPUT: Kernel, approximately half a page, predominantly mathematics (~95%), irreducible, interpretation-invariant expression of an idea.

STRUCTURE:
- Defaults to single exposition.
- Minimal explanatory text, targeted at LLMs.
- Star cites minimal.

FLOW: State governing claims and establish support with minimal exposition. Move directly between logically dependent results.

REGISTER:
- STE100-like clarity, precision, and terminological consistency.
- Extremely compressed, predominantly symbolic and declarative.
- Short sentences and minimal paragraphs, with little rhythmic variation.
- Impersonal, categorical where proven, qualified where conditional.

AVOID: explanatory padding, motivational digressions, redundant prose around notation.

REPORTS:
- Narrative Integrity: structural and mathematical fidelity.
- Factchecker: external facts and references.
- Claims Checker: proof validity, assumptions, and scope.


2. REPORT

OBJECTIVE: Expand kernel into complete, human expert readable, technically inspectable treatment without changing scientific message.
INPUT: kernel, supporting technical apparatus.
OUTPUT: Technical report, approximately 5 pages, ca. 50% math, 50% prose.

STRUCTURE:
- One hierarchical level by default.
- Inherit section headings and order from kernel.
- Expand dense mathematics into claims, derivations, interpretations, and qualifications.

FLOW: Develop each section from its governing result through mathematical substantiation to interpretation. Maintain continuity across sections without redundant summaries.

REGISTER:
- STE100-like clarity with disciplined scientific prose.
- Short-to-medium sentences with moderate variation.
- Short, relatively uniform paragraphs.
- Predominantly linear syntax, limited hypotaxis, restrained tone.

AVOID: redundant derivation recaps, scripted transitions between results, excessive pedagogical handholding.

REPORTS:
- Narrative Integrity: source fidelity, structure, notation, continuity.
- Factchecker: external facts and references.
- Claims Checker: derivations, assumptions, scientific support, scope.


3. PAPER

OBJECTIVE: Communicate scientific contribution through a clear conceptual argument supported by rigorous technical treatment.
INPUT: kernel, report, relevant literature, and paper scaffolding.
OUTPUT: Scientific paper, approximately 10-15 pages.

STRUCTURE:
- One or two hierarchical levels.
- Follow scaffolding if supplied, construct it first if missing.
- Provide section headers and bullet points specifying claims, support, and argumentative dependencies.
- Preserve the headline contribution and its supporting architecture.

FLOW: Abstract presents headline claims and shape of the support. Introduction establishes context, novelty, relevance. Develop conceptual argument before entering the technical treatment, typically around pages 3–5. Let formal results substantiate and refine the preceding exposition.

REGISTER:
- Established scientific voice with modest personal inflection.
- STE100-like terminological consistency and conceptual clarity.
- Medium-length sentences, substantial variation, moderately hypotactic and periodic.
- Medium-length paragraphs with deliberate rhythmic variation.
- Conceptually rich, explicit, authoritative but qualified.
- Restrained wit, occasional parentheticals, recognizable authorial voice.

AVOID: formulaic novelty claims, tutorial-style narration, repetition of the abstract in the introduction.

REPORTS:
- Narrative Integrity: fidelity to the scientific contribution, structural continuity, notation, internal consistency.
- Factchecker: literature, external evidence, references.
- Claims Checker: validity, novelty, evidentiary support, scope.


4. BOOK

OBJECTIVE: Integrate one or more individual contributions into coherent, sustained intellectual narrative.
INPUT: kernels, reports, papers, book guideline.
OUTPUT: book, typically around 300 pages unless otherwise specified.

STRUCTURE:
- Typically two to four hierarchical levels.
- Follow book guideline if supplied, construct if missing.
- Specify parts, chapters, sections, and supporting bullet points.
- Preserve the scientific contributions while developing their conceptual connections.

FLOW: Develop sustained intellectual progression across chapters. Introduce concepts where they become necessary, revisit them when new relationships emerge, progressively build toward synthesis. Integrate technical results into narrative without reproducing entire papers.

REGISTER:
- Scientific voice with stronger personal inflection.
- Explicit, interdisciplinary, conceptually adventurous.
- Medium-to-long sentences with high variability and occasional abrupt compression.
- Medium-to-long paragraphs with substantial rhythmic variation.
- Varied, hypotactic, periodic, occasionally parenthetical syntax.
- Confident but qualified, intellectually serious, sometimes playful or irreverent.
- Figurative language when it clarifies mechanisms or relationships.

AVOID: repetitive chapter previews, recaps that duplicate rather than develop, manufactured takeaways.

FOR ALL:

ESTABLISHED REGISTERS TO EMULATE:
- Herb Simon: analytical decomposition and systems reasoning.
- Frank Harary: precision and structural economy.
- Chris Alexander: patterns, connections, generative explanations.
- Ed Hutchins: situated reasoning, concrete mechanisms.

AVOID AI TROPES: manufactured symmetry, repetitive “buys” metaphor, formulaic antithesis, invented misconceptions, imaginary objections, grand compression claims, management-slide slogans, manufactured numbers of lessons, over-polished parallelism, mathematical anthropomorphism, forced economic metaphors, question-staged exposition, tutorial headings, announcements of importance, tour-guide transitions, excessive chapter previews, consultancy vocabulary, moralized precision, theatrical scope disclaimers, repeated caveats, recap inflation, generic admonitions, inflated significance, vague attribution, formulaic filler, decorative participles, mechanical uniformity, throat-clearing, semicolons, em dashes, mechanical anaphora, fake intimacy, boilerplate conclusions. 

TITLES: terse, no subtitles, no academese. 

EXTERNAL REPORTS:
- Narrative integrity check: fidelity to the scientific contribution, structural continuity, notation, internal consistency.
- Factcheck: literature, external evidence, references.
- Claims check: validity, novelty, evidentiary support, scope.
